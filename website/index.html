Know when the voice on the call isn't real.

SONIX listens to a live call and scores how likely the voice is AI-generated — a Green / Amber / Red signal that refreshes about twice a second, built on a frozen wav2vec2 front-end and a small trained head.

It does not block the call, hang up, or decide anything for you. It raises a signal. A human verifies.

About the project

Voice-clone phone scams, detected while the call is still happening

A convincing AI clone of someone's voice — a relative, an executive, a bank's IVR — is now cheap to produce and hard to tell apart from the real thing by ear. SONIX is a real-time detector built for exactly that moment: while the call is still open, not after the money is already gone.

Built for SIH26104, under the AICTE Cyber Security Cell problem statement, by team SONIX. It's aimed at anyone who takes calls they can't fully verify by voice alone — from an individual screening a "relative in trouble" call to an operator monitoring a support or verification line.

Architecture, one line

frozen wav2vec2 XLS-R (300M) → 1024-dim embedding, mean-pooled over a 4s window → small trained MLP head (~300k params) → risk score P(synthetic) in [0, 1]
  • Only the head is trained. The wav2vec2 front-end is frozen and never fine-tuned — that's what keeps training on a single GPU and real-time inference on a laptop GPU possible.
  • EER, not accuracy. The classes are heavily imbalanced, so equal error rate is the metric that's actually comparable to published baselines.
  • Thresholds are provisional. Green < 35%, Amber 35–65%, Red ≥ 65% were set on the clean ASVspoof benchmark and haven't been recalibrated on real recordings yet.
  • Indic-language robustness is being tested. A dedicated head (head_full_ho.pt) is trained on Indian-language speech with 12% of recordings held out of training to keep the test leak-free. Indic-language evaluation is in progress — no reproduced EER to quote yet.

End to end

Four ways audio gets in. One path decides.

Every capture source — a browser mic, a Google Meet tab, a VoIP call, or an uploaded file — feeds the exact same pipeline below. Nothing is scored, and nothing is even buffered past the consent gate, without an explicit human approval.

Click a step, or press play
  • The audio itself is never saved. It lives in a 4-second ring buffer and is continuously overwritten — only the events and scores go to disk.
  • Silence can't produce a verdict. The VAD gate scores RMS, peak and speech ratio per window and drops anything with no speech before it reaches the model.
  • Same code, every entry point. A live score and an offline score of the same audio agree by construction — there's one scoring implementation, not one per surface.

01 · Chrome extension

Install in Chrome

Manifest V3 · Chrome or Edge 116+ · captures a Google Meet tab

The extension streams the audio of a Google Meet tab to your local SONIX server and shows the live band in its popup and as an on-page overlay. It's a thin client — every bit of detection runs in the Python server, not in the browser.

  1. Open chrome://extensions
  2. Turn on Developer mode (top right)
  3. Click Load unpacked
  4. Select the extension/ folder inside this repository

The SONIX icon appears in the toolbar. Pin it — you'll want it visible during a call.

What it captures, and why

The tab's audio — the other participants. Not your microphone. That's the right way round: you're checking whether the person calling you is synthetic, not screening your own voice. Tab audio is the mixed output of the call, so a score applies to the 4-second window, not to a named speaker — per-speaker attribution would need diarisation, which SONIX doesn't do.

Permissions it asks for, and why

tabCapture

Captures the audio of the active Meet tab itself — the mechanism the "other participants, not your mic" design depends on.

offscreen

A Manifest V3 service worker can't touch audio directly. A hidden offscreen document is where getUserMedia and the AudioWorklet actually run.

activeTab

Confirms you're on a Meet tab before anything is captured, without requesting standing access to every site you visit.

Before you start monitoring, the local server must already be running (see Live Voice Detection). Tell the other participants they're being monitored — the overlay is visible on your screen only, Meet gives them no indication.

02 · Browser microphone

Live Voice Detection

Runs on this page — no separate window, no other route

Checking for a local SONIX server…

This starts a live call the same way the extension does — the server owns consent, windowing and scoring; this page is just the capture surface, the pairing approval and the live readout, all in one place.

  1. Start the server: python -m realtime.server --ws-port 8000 --mode webrtc
  2. Click Start capture below and allow microphone access
  3. A six-digit pairing code appears — click Approve on this device right below it
  4. Once approved, a new 4-second window fills roughly twice a second and the band goes live

If the server isn't running, Start capture will say so rather than silently doing nothing — there's nothing to connect to. If it's running but no trained head was loaded (no --ckpt), capture, pairing and consent all still work live — the band just reports "Scoring unavailable" instead of a number, honestly, rather than showing a made-up one. Microphone access needs a secure context — this works over localhost, not over a plain LAN IP.
