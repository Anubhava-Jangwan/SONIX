Weighting: why this head might matter most here

This is a fixed, readable rule over cheap audio features (bandwidth, estimated SNR) — not a trained router. It never decides the verdict: the verdict above is the maximum score across every head, so a blind spot in one head can't silently pass audio another head would have flagged. The weights only explain which head's training makes it most relevant to this clip's channel and noise conditions.

Why score with every head?
  1. No single model wins everywhere. Heads trained with different augmentation disagree on the same audio — this makes that visible instead of hiding it behind one number.
  2. Ensembling is free here. The 300M-parameter front-end runs once; each extra head is 262,657 parameters scored on an embedding already computed.
  3. Disagreement is information. Heads agreeing raises confidence; heads splitting flags audio worth a human listen.
  4. Safety floor. The verdict is the maximum across heads, so a blind spot in one head can't silently pass audio another head would have caught.
